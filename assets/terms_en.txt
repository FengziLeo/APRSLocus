1. About the APRS Protocol

1.1 APRS (Automatic Packet Reporting System) is a real-time tactical digital communication protocol developed by Bob Bruninga (WB4APR) for exchanging information among a large number of stations over a wide area. APRS was originally based on the AX.25 packet radio protocol and operates on VHF frequencies (144.390 MHz in North America, 144.800 MHz in Europe) at 1200 bps using AFSK (Audio Frequency Shift Keying).

1.2 The APRS protocol specifications cover various data formats, including position reports, weather data, messages, telemetry, status, objects, items, queries, and bulletins. APRS v1.0 was released in June 2000, v1.1 was updated in July 2004, and the protocol continues to evolve.

1.3 APRS differs from conventional packet radio in four key aspects: integration of maps and other data displays; a one-to-many broadcast protocol for real-time updates; generic digital relays that need no prior knowledge of network topology; and, since 1997, a transparent Internet backbone connecting users worldwide.

1.4 As an APRS client, this software sends and receives data in accordance with the above protocol specifications. All data you send through this software follows the APRS protocol format and may be received, stored, and forwarded by other nodes on the global APRS network.

1.5 You should be aware that the position beacons you send through this software, and the continuous track they form, may constitute "movement tracks" — sensitive personal information under the Personal Information Protection Law of the People's Republic of China. Disclosure or unlawful use of such information may put personal safety at risk or harm human dignity. The sole purpose for which this software processes such information is to provide the APRS communication function itself; no alternative exists that delivers the same APRS function without uploading your position, and that is why processing this sensitive personal information is necessary.

2. About the APRS-IS Internet Service

2.1 APRS-IS (APRS-Internet Service) is an Internet backbone network maintained by volunteer amateur radio operators worldwide, connecting APRS radio networks around the globe.

2.2 This software is a pure client tool and does not operate any APRS servers. All APRS data you send and receive through this software is transmitted via third-party APRS-IS public networks. We assume no responsibility for the availability, accuracy, or security of those networks.

2.3 The APRS-IS network is operated and maintained by volunteers. All users shall comply with the following basic rules:

· Every APRS-IS packet may be forwarded to radio networks, so beacon rates and packet content must comply with RF usage norms;

· Beacon rates should follow reasonable RF frequency standards (mobile stations no less than 1 minute, weather stations no less than 5 minutes, fixed stations no less than 10 minutes, local infrastructure no less than 20 minutes);

· It is prohibited to inject non-amateur-radio content into APRS-IS while masquerading as an amateur station;

· Access to APRS-IS is limited to operators holding a valid amateur radio callsign.

2.4 We do not provide, operate, or recommend any APRS server address, and we do not provide any Internet service such as APRS-IS, iGate, data upload, data relay, or data storage. The software ships with no server address or port: on first launch and under default settings it connects to no APRS-IS, iGate, or other APRS network server. Whether to connect, and to where, is entirely yours to enter and decide. Before you first enable a server connection, the software shows a Data & Network Notice, and connects only after you confirm it.

2.5 We have no affiliation, agency, or cooperation with any third-party server. If you configure and connect to one yourself, you must confirm that the server and your use of it comply with the law, and you bear all responsibility arising from cross-border data transfer, personal-data processing, radio regulation, and the third party's terms of service.

2.6 The APRS-IS Passcode mechanism only serves operator verification within the amateur radio community. It is not a means of encrypting, anonymizing, or protecting location data. Do not treat it as a security barrier.

4. Data Storage and Privacy

4.1 APRS is by nature a public position-reporting system. APRS data is transmitted in plaintext over amateur radio frequencies and the Internet, and can be received and decoded by anyone. APRS is never encrypted and never private.

4.2 Location tracks, weather data, and messages collected by the APRS-IS network are public data. All APRS data you send through this software may be received, stored, forwarded, and publicly archived by other nodes on the APRS-IS network as well as third-party services. Some public servers retain location data for 180 days or longer; that is decided by each server operator.

4.3 If you do not wish your location to be known to anyone in the world, please do not send it via APRS or the Internet.

4.4 Local Data Storage: To ensure normal operation, this software stores the following data locally on your device:

· Beacon history and track points;

· Sent and received messages;

· Address book (callsigns and notes);

· Custom notes and theme settings for stations;

· Software configuration parameters (e.g., server address, beacon interval, map layer preferences, etc.).

4.5 All the above data is stored only locally on your device by default and is not automatically uploaded to any server controlled by us or any third party. You can delete this local data by using the "Clear Data" function within the software or by uninstalling the software.

4.6 By default the software records only the history track belonging to your own callsign. "Record all received stations" is an explicit option you must turn on yourself; it is not a default. History tracks and exported GPX files contain precise timestamped coordinate sequences and may likewise constitute movement-track sensitive personal information.

4.7 The limits of deletion: the "delete" functions in the software clear the corresponding local data from this software's database and cache. Backups and GPX files that you exported, saved elsewhere, or shared by other means are outside what this software can delete, and you must dispose of them yourself. When you use the backup or export features, the data leaves the software's control as a file, and the software cannot delete it, withdraw it, or limit its further distribution.

4.8 We do not actively collect, store, or process your personal data. The APRS data you send through this software is transmitted directly via third-party APRS-IS networks and does not pass through any server under our control.

4.9 You are responsible for safeguarding your device against unauthorised access to your local data. The development team assumes no liability for data disclosure resulting from loss, theft, or use of your device by others.

4.10 Do not send state secrets, trade secrets, personal privacy, or sensitive location information over APRS. APRS data may travel in the clear over radio and public networks and may be received, stored, or publicly archived by third parties.

6.2 The main data flows are as follows:

(1) APRS-IS connection: triggered only after you complete the separate consent in Appendix 1 and turn it on manually; data flows to the APRS-IS server you entered; it may send your callsign, position beacons, and message content.

(2) Map tile loading: triggered when you open the map view; data flows to the map provider you selected (Amap, Baidu, Tencent, OpenStreetMap, CARTO, Esri, and others); typically a map request and your IP address are sent.

(3) Translation: triggered when you actively use translation; data flows to the translation endpoint you selected; the text to be translated is sent.

(4) LiveTrack sharing: triggered when you add a sharing link yourself; data flows to the sharing service you chose (such as Garmin); location data is sent, depending on that service.

(5) Update check: triggered on launch; data flows to GitHub or an update server; the current version number is sent, and your callsign and position are not uploaded.

These communications happen only when you actively use the corresponding feature, and not otherwise; each provider processes data under its own terms and privacy policy.

6.3 In addition, so that the software works properly, on launch the app requests a few small static files from our official site and public code-hosting services: the in-app notice, the remote configuration list, the APRS device-identifier database, and the member list. These requests only download; they never upload your callsign, position, messages, or install ID. Apart from these, the app performs no background network communication.
